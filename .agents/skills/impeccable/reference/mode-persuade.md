# Persuade and Experience

`impeccable concept-seed --mode persuade` prints the two sections below as its MODE RULES block. On Persuade and Experience surfaces (such as the landing splash screen, victory/game-over screens, celebratory milestone modals, room lobby, and store in `vtcoon`), the visitor judges the impression and feeling, so the visual world may own and structure the presentation.

## Directions

The surface may carry its native grammar across navigation, content, controls, and states, within platform usability conventions.

For **Persuade**, the opening must make the offer intelligible and desirable, expose a clear action, and demonstrate something unique. The action is the one visitors came to take: **play game, join room, purchase, bid**. Conversion lives inside the form's own vocabulary: a hook that lands clearly, a visible primary action, a legible reading order.

For **Experience** (splash screens, victory/game-over screens, celebratory modals), the work itself leads from the first viewport. The world's material, its typography, and its mood set the stage directly.

Read every candidate and challenger through that license: the world may structure the surface, but it may never make the primary action unfindable, the text unreadable, or the price ambiguous. When a candidate's world is an artifact (a poster, a game board, a catalog), take its physical hierarchy and surface qualities, not a literal scan of the artifact.

Write the first viewport with its hierarchy clear: what catches the eye first, what confirms the offer, the primary action and its affordance, and where the world's material commits most strongly. Name the visual anchor concretely so the comp can render it.

## Comps & Layouts

The visual world may be the carrier of the page, never merely a flat picture of it. Material coverage is intentional: the world's material is the ground and major surfaces, its typography sets the headlines, and its ornament frames the content.

The world carries the page's parts and never absorbs them:
- An action on a button or plaque must remain plainly clickable (`active:translate-y-[3px]` tactile response).
- Text must read with high contrast against whatever sits behind it.
- Sections keep a steady vertical rhythm and clear spacing.
- The primary call-to-action is unmistakable.
- Avoid both extremes: an un-interactive painted poster, and a generic template with random stickers.

Comps two and three explore different emotional registers within the world: one may lead with dramatic scale and deep atmosphere, another with tactile detail and craftsmanship, a third with dynamic rhythm. Each must be distinctly recognizable from the others at thumbnail view.

Self-check every render: Does it compel attention while keeping the user's next step immediately obvious? If the world overwhelms the primary call-to-action, restore functional contrast to the action button and reading layer.
