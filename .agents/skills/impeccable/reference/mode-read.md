# Read

On a Read surface the visitor came to understand something, so the page is first a document of its kind, framed by the world.

## Directions

The reader's question leads, and comprehension and wayfinding stay intact at every step. Documentation reads as documentation, a guide as a guide, an article as an article, a changelog as a changelog. The world owns the frame and serves the column.

The frame is the masthead, the navigation rail, the ground, the title and section openers, and the way code, tables, and callouts are set: there the world's palette, type, and signature move commit fully, so the page could belong to no other product. The reading column inside it stays calm: a reading face, real contrast, a real measure, nothing performing behind the text. The world never supplies the reading order or replaces the navigation with something a reader has to learn.

Write the first viewport as the document's shell first: the navigation and where the reader stands in it, the title and the opening that answers or frames the reader's question, the reading column, and the way forward (contents, section headings, next and previous). Then name where the world owns the frame and where the signature move lives.

## Comps & Layouts

The document renders as the reader meets it: a frame the world owns, in the direction's committed palette and type, around a calm reading column at a real measure (about 60 to 75 characters with body text near 16px; in a mobile viewport, the readable width), with headings in a clear hierarchy and wayfinding that carries the reader through.

Body text is set in a reading face with real contrast against its ground; monospace is for code, parameters, and small labels, never running text or headings by default. Code, tables, notes, and figures sit in standard forms readers already know, set in the world's type and palette.

Self-check: Can a reader tell where they are and where to go next, and read the body comfortably? If the render reads as an artifact of the world before it reads as a document, the world has reached the column; pull it back to the frame.
